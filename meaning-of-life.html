Purpose, hope and being human

Life feels like it should mean more.

Wanting more than survival, work and distraction is not ingratitude. It may be one of the most honest things about you.

← Back to all questions

Why does ordinary life sometimes feel too small?

Work, money, relationships, pleasure and achievement are real goods. But none of them can safely carry the whole weight of a human life. They change, disappoint us or eventually end.

Christianity begins with a larger claim: your life is not an accident. You are made deliberately, known personally and called to love God and other people.

Meaning is received as well as made

You can choose goals, but Catholic faith says you do not have to invent your value. Your worth comes before productivity, status, success or anyone else’s approval.

A useful question:

What would you still call meaningful even if nobody applauded it, posted about it or paid you for it?

Purpose often arrives as the next faithful step

You may not receive a complete life plan. Meaning is often found in concrete things: keeping a promise, caring for someone, telling the truth, praying, forgiving, making something good or serving a community.

The Christian story says those ordinary acts can participate in something eternal.
