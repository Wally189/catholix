An honest place to begin

I’m not sure God exists.

You do not have to manufacture certainty. Doubt can be a serious, reasonable starting point rather than a failure.

← Back to all questions

What are you actually unsure about?

People mean different things when they say they do not believe in God. You may think science has made God unnecessary. You may have seen suffering that makes belief feel impossible. You may simply never have experienced anything that seemed religious.

It helps to name the strongest version of your real objection rather than arguing with a weaker one.

Faith is not meant to switch off your mind

Catholic Christianity claims that reason and faith belong together. Questions about why anything exists, why the universe is intelligible, whether moral truth is real and why human beings hunger for meaning are not scientific gaps. They are philosophical questions worth examining carefully.

Try this without pretending:

Write down the strongest reason you doubt—and the strongest reason you still wonder. Give both sides a fair hearing.

Prayer can begin as an experiment

You do not need certainty to pray honestly. A first prayer could be: “God, I do not know whether you are there. If you are, help me recognise what is true.”

That is not surrendering your intelligence. It is becoming open to evidence that may include experience, conscience, beauty, reason and encounter with other people.
